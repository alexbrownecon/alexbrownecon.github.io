## Q1 — What drives the research (the common thread)

- "The common theme is predicting and explaining human behavior in novel ways
  using formalized theory."
- Positions himself between two poles: "a fully empirical approach that just
  documents deviations" and "a full theoretical one that derives predictions
  without robust verification."
- Respects both extremes — has written papers in each mode — "but the ultimate
  goal is to get to a unified theory that explains human behavior with
  economics formality."

## Q2 — Positioning: what each audience should take away

**Core identity claims (his words, lightly cleaned):**
- Breadth of tools: "ability to use many tools in economics — theory, empirics,
  experimental design, expertise in past literature."
- "A real desire to work with high-brow theorists and test their work; a love
  of economic theory despite frequently testing and finding failures...
  behavioral economic theory is the solution."
- Nuanced stances he owns publicly: "strong support of loss aversion but
  realization it has been over-applied"; "recognition of failures of bounded
  rationality in things like contingent thinking."
- "Expertise in lab experimental design, but willing to branch to alternative
  means when necessary."
- Translation to practice: "willingness to collaborate with applied researchers
  in fields like traffic and chemical engineering, work with policy makers at
  the CFPB toward a greater good — translating high-brow econ theory to
  applications."

**One-line takeaways per audience:**
- Hiring/search committees: "will work on high-brow econ topics for publication
  in [top] journals but also get grants."
- Prospective students: "need an economic theory background to work with me."

## Q3 — Tone

- "Probably direct... it's an arms race with self-promotion now. It isn't my
  skill, but I don't want to hurt myself by being too understated (I have
  before)."
- "Warm towards students is good too."
- Implication for drafting: state accomplishments plainly and confidently, no
  hedging or false modesty — but the warmth is reserved for the
  student-facing text. Direct ≠ salesy; the record is strong enough that
  plain declarative sentences do the promoting.

## Q4 — Teaching (substantive: placeholder stays, with real content)

- "The classes I teach are areas where I have considerable expertise from my
  research. My sincere goal is to have my students learn; I want to distill my
  knowledge."
- Courses are "demanding" with "high expectations that students will pick up a
  lot of material; they are also math-intensive." Has "tried many ways to make

  best students the opportunity to find more material. As a student eager to
  learn at a big institution myself, I was frustrated when the material felt
  bounded; the best instructors would give me more so I could go off in my own
  areas."

  coauthor with them at undergraduate, MS, and of course grad levels. My first
  paper — Brown and Kagel — is from undergraduate work. It changed my life."
